Pay the crew tonight.
In dollars. On Bitcoin.

After a show, the organizer has to pay the sound engineer, the lighting crew and the artist. With ScratchMyPay they type it in plain words. A local AI drafts a validated payment flow, a person approves the exact plan, and it settles as an RGB stablecoin transfer on Bitcoin, exactly once, with a receipt both sides can check. Payout details never leave the organizer's computer.

How it worksDescribe → Review → Approve → Track

Describe

“Pay the sound engineer 25 tUSDT for Saturday's show.” The crew member's payment request is pasted or scanned as a QR.

Review

QVAC, running on this computer, turns the sentence into a typed request; a pinned template compiles it into a flow that is validated before anything runs. The network cost comes live from Tether WDK.

Approve

A person approves the exact plan. The approval is bound to the plan's fingerprint and the flow revision; change either and it is void.

Track

Planned → approved → sending → sent → crew wallet checked it → settled. A durable journal guarantees it is paid at most once, even if the computer dies mid-send.

Why RGB, QVAC and WDKeach one does a job the product needs

RGB

Dollars that settle on Bitcoin

A test stablecoin (TUSDT) issued on Mutinynet, witness invoices, consignments delivered to the crew wallet and validated client-side. Wrong-network invoices are refused; the crew wallet settles on its own once the payment confirms.

QVAC

AI that stays on the laptop

Qwen3-8B via QVAC drafts payouts and builds receive-side flows in the editor (pay-me QR page, treasury check), adapting saved templates from a local library. It never touches the money path: it proposes, a person approves.

WDK

A treasury with a business model

Tether WDK runs the treasury: live fee quotes on the Review screen, a pre-show “can we cover tonight's payouts?” check, and the pay-to-enter door: 1,000 sats buys a 24-hour pass. Revenue in, network fees out, one balance.

n2link

Flows you can see

Every payout is a visible flow in n2link, a Rust, Node-RED-compatible runtime. Payout nodes only run inside the managed tab, behind an approval gate; the deploy guard refuses anything else.

Honest notes

This is a hackathon build on Mutinynet with test money. Approval is a typed name and a checkbox, not a signature. The pay-to-enter door proves payment, not identity. Prior work is declared: the n2link runtime and the RGB Bill wallet adapter existed before the event; everything else was built from Oct 15 with the organizers' approval to start early.
